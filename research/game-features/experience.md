# Experience and player development

Researched 2026-11-08, built 2026-11-08 to 2026-11-10 (`engine/src/development/`). The questions: how do real players change over a career, how does FIFA/EA FC career mode make players grow, and how should experience (XP) and ageing work so the user's players can drift from the real ratings. The age curves are measured from our own ten editions (FIFA 15 to FC 24, `male_players.csv`) with `age_curves.py` in this folder (rerun it if the data changes). Numbers that live in code (`AGE_BONUS`, `MEAN_CHANGE`, the XP constants) are not copied here: read them there.

## 1. How real players change (measured from the data)

Mean change in overall from one edition to the next, by age at the first edition (n = players with a next edition, so players who left the data are not counted; sd of a single player's change about 2-3 points at every age):

| Age | 17 | 19 | 21 | 23 | 25 | 27 | 29 | 31 | 33 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change | +3.0 | +2.6 | +2.0 | +1.2 | +0.7 | +0.4 | +0.1 | -0.2 | -0.5 | -0.7 |

- **Potential matters most while young.** Age 18-20: +2.2 with a gap of 5 to potential, +2.4 with 10, +2.8 with 15, +3.3 with 20 or more. Age 21-23: +1.3 with no gap, +1.5 with 5, +2.0 with 10 or more. Age 24-26: +0.6 with no gap, +1.1 with a gap of 5 or more.
- **Position:** goalkeepers develop later and decline later (24-26: +1.0; 30-32: +0.1; outfield players 30-32: -0.2 to -0.3; 36 and over: GK -0.5, attackers -0.9). Outfield positions are close to each other.
- **Attributes age differently.** Mean change per edition by age band (17-20 / 21-24 / 25-28 / 29-32 / 33+): stamina +2.4 / +1.5 / +0.2 / -1.1 / -2.3; sprint speed +1.1 / +0.3 / -0.7 / -1.7 / -2.4; acceleration +1.1 / +0.3 / -0.6 / -1.6 / -2.2; agility +2.0 / +0.9 / +0.1 / -0.6 / -1.1; finishing +1.8 / +1.1 / +0.4 / 0.0 / -0.4; short passing +2.6 / +1.4 / +0.6 / +0.1 / -0.1; vision +2.4 / +1.6 / +0.8 / +0.4 / +0.4; composure +3.0 / +2.0 / +1.1 / +0.5 / +0.2; strength +2.2 / +1.3 / +0.7 / +0.3 / -0.1. Physical attributes (pace, stamina, agility) fall from the late twenties; mental and passing attributes keep rising until the early thirties. The Swedish and Champions League studies report the same: physical performance falls after 30 while passing ability rises ([Frontiers in Psychology 2019](https://www.doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00076), [PMC6360147](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6360147)), with an overall peak around 25-27, goalkeepers and centre-backs later (about 31).
- Caveat: the data is FIFA's rating of players, which is its own scouts' view, not a measurement of ability.

## 2. What EA FC does

- Until FC 24 growth was mostly fixed: age, potential and (for the user's own players) a development plan or training. FC 25 added *dynamic potential* and **Match XP**: players earn XP from their actions and performance in matches, reaching or missing their potential depending on playing time and form; training intensity speeds growth but costs stamina ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/how-dynamic-potential-work-ea-fc-25-explained), [Space Coast Daily](https://spacecoastdaily.com/2025/04/fc-25-career-mode-gets-smarter-how-the-new-player-growth-system-works/); third-party guides, details unverified). EA's older notes say development speed depends on age, abilities, potential, form and playtime ([EA FIFA 21 career mode deep dive](https://www.ea.com/news/pitch-notes-fifa-21-career-mode-deep-dive)). Players over 30 decline whatever they do, and development plans only slow it ([EA FIFA 23 player development](https://www.ea.com/news/fifa-23-player-development)). Reports for FC 27 say form, morale and match fitness will feed ratings and a better coach will judge potential more accurately ([Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/ea-fc-27-career-mode-new-features); pre-release, unverified).
- Player complaints: a decline driven only by age feels unfair to a player who is in form; a decline with no way to slow it is what most dislike (forum posts, opinion).

## 3. What was built

Decisions: only the user's club develops (everyone else follows the real data); match XP only (no training yet); XP is worth less to a high overall; potential is a soft limit; XP fills a bar and a full bar is a level.

### Match XP and levels

- An experience model is two pure functions, `match_xp` and `level_points`, chosen by `ExperienceModel { None, MatchBased }`. Every match gives each player XP, added to his bar (`Player::xp`) and to his season total. When the bar reaches 100 he levels up and the XP over the line stays. The GUI calls `World::level_up_club` after every match the user plays and says who levelled up.
- **Match XP** (`match_based.rs`): 1 for turning up, 4 x the share of the match played, 3 x form, 2 for player of the match, at most 10; times a factor that falls with overall (1 up to 54, 0.3 at 90 and over); then, for a young player, times an age bonus (`AGE_BONUS`: 2.0 up to 20, 1.8 to 23, 1.5 to 26) that comes after the 10 limit, so the youngest can get up to 20. Form is the match rating against what an uneventful match is worth (`rating::neutral_rating`) plus what a regular of his line gets above that (forwards 0.33, goalkeepers 0.32, midfielders 0.16, defenders 0.12, measured over a simulated FIFA 15 season of 5300 regulars). XP is never negative.
- **Measured** over a simulated season (before the age bonus): 4.0-4.3 XP a match for overalls 50-69, 3.3 for 70s, 2.4 for 80s, 2.2 for 90s; the last match of every player ranged from 1 to 10, mostly 2 to 5. A regular at 4 XP a match fills the bar about once every 25 matches. Age was not in the XP at all at first, and a young bench player (3 XP a cameo) barely grew; that is why the bonus exists.
- **A level** gives 1.0 overall point to a player of 21 or younger below his potential, falling to a quarter by 33; past potential it is worth exp(-points over / 6) of that (a soft limit: a player who keeps winning keeps rising, slowly). Potential itself is not raised.
- **Applied** to every attribute that counts for his position, each by the level's points (rounded at random so the average is exact), and to the overall by what those are worth (`overall_change`). A test checks the overall and the attributes move together.
- Measured (simulated careers from FIFA 15, a mid-table Premier League club, 8 seasons): about 11 to 17 levels a season over the squad. Not tuned against anything: there is no real figure for how much playing time changes a career.

### The summer (ageing)

A new season used to give every player, the user's included, the real change between two editions on top of his rating (`follow_real_change`). For the user's squad that was noise: attributes jumped by what EA's scouts decided for the real player. Now the user's club is left out of the real changes (`advance_ratings(conn, world, Some(club))` leaves their overall, potential, attributes and positions alone) and changes by its own rules once when a season starts (`World::age_club`, `AgeingModel { None, ByAge }`, `development/ageing.rs`, after the transfers and before the retirement draw).

- **The table** (`MEAN_CHANGE`) is the mean overall change over a summer by age: growth from +3.0 at 16-17 down to +0.2 at 27, zero at 28-30, then slow decline from 31, more at 33-34 and a lot after 35 (-2.4 at 35, -4.0 from 38). It declines faster than the measured averages in section 1 (-0.2 at 31, -0.7 at 35), which leave out the players who dropped out of the data; the user asked for it ("slowly at 31, a bit more at 33 and 34, a lot after 35").
- **Potential** matters to growth only: 0.6 of the table's with no gap, 1.0 with a gap of 10, at most 1.4; past potential it falls with exp(gap / 6). A decline does not depend on potential. **Goalkeepers** change as an outfield player two years younger. A **random part** (two even draws, up to 2.9 either way, sd about 1.2) is added.
- **A real rise is never held back**: `advance_ratings` keeps each of the user's players' real change in overall (`Player::real_change`) and `age_club` uses it instead of its own change when it is a bigger rise. A real decline or a smaller rise changes nothing. Players who really got good should not be held back; a decline we choose is ours.
- **No luck-only declines**: a player the table says is growing or steady (up to 30, goalkeepers up to 32) never goes down in a summer. Without this the random draw alone took 1 to 2 points off prime players (a 27-year-old goalkeeper lost 2), which felt like an attack. The cost is a small rise in the average growth of those players.
- **Positions and the overall must stay in step.** A player's rating at another position is his overall plus the difference between that position's formula and the formula of the position his overall belongs to (`lineup::rating_at`), so his overall must keep matching his attributes there. On real data overall and formula agree within about 3 points (98% of players); a gap of 15 appeared for the user's players when the data gave them a new position (a winger turned right-back) while their attributes stayed, and a right-back rated 92 at left midfield. Fixes: the user's players keep their positions, and levels and ageing move the attributes of the position the overall belongs to (`lineup::overall_position`). Over five simulated seasons the biggest rating above a player's overall anywhere on the pitch is 2 points, instead of 8. Cleaner option, not built: make attributes the only truth (one fixed offset per player at load, overall derived).
- **On top** of the summer come the levels from match XP. In a simulated 5-season career of a Manchester United squad they added only +0.1 to +0.9 overall a player a season, because most of the squad plays little, so the summer table is most of a player's change.
- **Shown**: the line after "Start next season" says how many players improved and declined; the squad view's "this season" includes the summer change.

### Not built

Training XP, potential rising with levels, a growth profile per attribute and age (every attribute of the position moves equally, where real players lose pace before passing; section 1), levels for clubs other than the user's, and ageing of other clubs' players after FC 24 (they stop changing; M5).
